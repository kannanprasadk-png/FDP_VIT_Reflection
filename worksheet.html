Step 1: Choose one real task

Pick a task you actually set in a course you teach: an essay, a case analysis, a lab report, a reading response, a quiz. Then write its learning outcome in one sentence, starting with a verb the student performs.

Step 2: Run the offloading audit

List the steps a student takes to complete your task. For each step, ask the audit question, then decide:

Offload The step is only a route to the outcome, and the learner loses no practice the outcome depends on.
Protect The step is where the outcome lives. Keep it unaided, or allow AI only after the learner has made their own attempt.
Redesign The step matters, but AI makes it easy to skip. Add process evidence, an oral check or a guardrailed prompt.

The same step can get different decisions in different courses. It depends on the outcome you wrote in Step 1, so keep that in view.

Step 3: Choose the lane

A task is either secure, meaning it verifies what a student can do unaided, or open, meaning it teaches and AI is permitted. This follows the two-lane approach used at the University of Sydney. A secure task does not always ban AI, but it is supervised so you can trust the result.

Question about your taskAnswer
Does this task certify that the student can do something unaided? (if yes: Secure)
Would a weak result here carry a high cost, such as a final grade or a professional standard? (if yes: Secure)
Is this mainly where students practise and get feedback? (if yes: Open)
Is working with AI a skill graduates in this field are expected to have? (if yes: Open, and ask students to explain how they used AI)
Answer the four questions to see a suggested lane.
